Emergency care

Knocked-out tooth: what to do in the next hour

An adult tooth that has been knocked out can often be put back and saved, but the clock is running from the moment it leaves the socket. The cells on the root begin to die within minutes if the tooth dries out. Read the steps below, do them, then call. Thirty minutes to an hour is the window that gives the best chance.

What to do right now

  1. Pick it up by the crown

    The white part you chew with. Do not touch the root. The root surface is what has to survive.

  2. If it is dirty, rinse briefly

    A few seconds under milk or saline, or cool water if nothing else is available. Do not scrub, do not wipe, do not use soap or disinfectant, and do not let it dry.

  3. Put it back in the socket

    Facing the right way, with gentle finger pressure, then bite softly on a clean cloth or gauze to hold it. This is the best possible storage. It is easier than it sounds and it is what gives the tooth its best chance.

  4. If it will not go back, keep it wet

    In a small container of milk, or in saline, or tucked inside the cheek if the person is an adult who will not swallow it. Not in water, which damages the root cells, and never dry in a tissue.

  5. Call and come straight in

    Call us on the way. If it is outside our hours, go to the nearest emergency room with the tooth and call us for follow-up.

Baby teeth are different: do not put them back

A knocked-out baby tooth is not reimplanted, because pushing it back can damage the adult tooth forming underneath. Stop the bleeding with a clean cloth, keep the tooth to show us, and call for a visit to check the socket and the neighboring teeth. If you are not sure whether it is a baby or adult tooth, keep it in milk and treat it as an adult tooth until we see it.

What is usually behind it

  • Sports without a mouthguard: basketball, soccer, lacrosse, hockey, skateboarding, cycling.
  • Falls, especially in toddlers learning to walk and in older adults.
  • Car and bicycle accidents.
  • Fights and assaults.
  • Teeth already loosened by advanced gum disease, which come out with little force.

What happens at the visit

If the tooth is already back in the socket, we check its position with an X-ray and adjust it. If you brought it in milk, we clean the socket gently, reinsert the tooth and confirm the position. Then the tooth is splinted to its neighbors with a thin wire and bonding for a week or two so the ligament can reattach. You go home with instructions for a soft diet, gentle cleaning and pain relief, and we check for a tetanus booster if the injury was dirty.

A reimplanted adult tooth almost always needs root canal therapy within the following week or two, because the nerve does not survive the injury. That is planned at the first visit, not a surprise later. The tooth is then watched at intervals for a year or more.

How it is treated

Reimplantation and splinting

The tooth is placed back, checked on X-ray and held with a flexible splint for seven to fourteen days.

Learn more

Root canal therapy

Done within a week or two to prevent infection as the nerve dies.

Learn more

Repairing damage to neighbors

Chipped or loosened adjacent teeth are bonded, splinted or watched as needed.

If the tooth cannot be saved

If it was dry too long or the socket is fractured, the gap is planned for an implant or bridge once healing allows.

Outside our hours

We see patients 7 AM to 8 PM on weekdays and 8 AM to 1 PM on weekends. If a tooth is knocked out at 10 PM, do the steps above and go to the nearest hospital emergency room with the tooth, back in the socket or in milk. An emergency physician can reimplant and splint it. Then call us at 7 AM and we take over the follow-up, including the root canal and the monitoring. See dental emergencies after hours.

Prevention is a mouthguard

A custom sports mouthguard, made from an impression so it fits well enough that it actually gets worn, costs a small fraction of a knocked-out front tooth and the years of treatment that follow. We make them for children and adults, including over braces.

Straight answers

How long does the tooth have?

The best outcomes are when the tooth is back in the socket within thirty minutes, and good outcomes are still common within an hour if it has been kept in milk. After that the chances fall steadily, but it is still worth bringing the tooth; we will try.

What if the tooth is in pieces?

A tooth broken in the fall is treated as a broken tooth rather than reimplanted. Bring every piece. A root fractured in the socket is assessed on X-ray; some can be splinted and heal, some cannot.

Will the tooth look and work normally afterward?

Often, yes, for many years. A reimplanted tooth needs a root canal and monitoring, and some are eventually lost to root resorption, a slow process we watch for on X-rays. Even then, keeping the tooth for years preserves the bone for a future implant.

Can I put a tooth back in a child's mouth?

An adult tooth in a child of six or older, yes, following the steps above. A baby tooth, no. If unsure, keep it in milk and come in.
