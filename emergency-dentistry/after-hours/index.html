Emergency care

A dental emergency after hours

Our hours cover more of the week than most dental offices: 7 AM to 8 PM Monday to Friday and 8 AM to 1 PM on Saturday and Sunday. That still leaves nights and weekend afternoons. Here is what to do if a tooth breaks at 10 PM or starts throbbing on a Sunday afternoon, and when a hospital is the right place to go.

What is usually behind an after-hours call

  • A toothache that has built through the evening and will not let you sleep.
  • A crown or filling that came out at dinner.
  • A tooth chipped or broken on something hard.
  • Swelling that appeared over a few hours.
  • A knocked-out tooth from an evening game or a fall.
  • Bleeding after an extraction earlier in the day.

What to do right now

  1. Book the first available slot online

    Online scheduling runs around the clock and shows real availability. The earliest weekday slots start at 7 AM, so a 10 PM problem can usually be seen within hours of the office opening. Or call 703-263-8719 at opening time.

  2. For pain

    Rinse with warm salt water, floss gently around the tooth, take an over-the-counter pain reliever exactly as labeled if it is safe for you, and sleep with your head raised. Do not put aspirin on the gum. See the toothache guide.

  3. For a lost crown or filling

    Keep the crown. Temporary dental cement from a pharmacy can seat it or fill the cavity until morning. Avoid cold, sweet and chewing on that side. See lost filling or crown.

  4. For a broken tooth

    Keep the pieces in milk, cover a sharp edge with wax or sugar-free gum, and do not chew on it. See broken or chipped tooth.

  5. For bleeding after an extraction

    Bite firmly on clean gauze or a damp tea bag for twenty minutes without checking. Sit upright. If it has not slowed after two rounds, that is an emergency room visit.

  6. For a knocked-out adult tooth

    Hold it by the crown, rinse briefly, put it back in the socket or in milk, and go to the nearest emergency room now. Do not wait for us to open. See knocked-out tooth.

What happens when we open

Tell whoever answers, or note in your online booking, that it is urgent and what happened. We keep room for emergencies during our hours. You are seen, the cause is found with an exam and a digital X-ray, the pain is dealt with first, and the lasting fix is planned. If you went to a hospital overnight, bring any paperwork and let us know what was done; we pick up from there, including the root canal that a reimplanted tooth will need.

Straight answers

Do you treat emergencies at night?

No. Treatment happens during our hours, which run until 8 PM on weekdays and include weekend mornings. Online booking never closes, so you can secure the first morning slot at any hour.

Will the emergency room fix my tooth?

Usually not. A hospital treats infection, pain, bleeding and injuries, and can reimplant a knocked-out tooth, but definitive dental treatment is done by a dentist afterward. For a toothache or a lost crown, you will generally do better booking our first 7 AM slot than sitting in a hospital waiting room.

Can I book an emergency appointment online?

Yes. Choose the earliest available slot and note what the problem is. If you can, also call when we open so we can confirm the plan.

I am not a patient yet. Can I still come in for an emergency?

Yes. New patients are welcome. Register on your phone through the online forms on the way in and bring your insurance card if you have one.
