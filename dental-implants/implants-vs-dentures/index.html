Dental implants

Implants or dentures? An honest comparison

Both replace missing teeth. Beyond that they are different in almost every way: how they feel, how they chew, what they do to the jaw over time, how long they take, and what they cost now and later. There is no universally right answer, and there is a middle ground that suits many people. Here is the comparison we go through in the chair.

The bone question

This is the difference that matters most over a lifetime and is least visible at the start. Bone stays where it is loaded. Tooth roots load the jaw; so do implants. A denture resting on the gum does not, and the ridge beneath it shrinks year by year. That is why dentures loosen, why they need relining, and why a long-term denture wearer's face changes shape. Implants stop that process where they are placed. Someone choosing dentures at sixty should know what the ridge will look like at seventy-five.

The cost question, honestly

Dentures cost far less to make. Over fifteen or twenty years, though, they need relines, repairs and usually at least one remake, plus adhesive, and the chewing limitations have their own costs in diet and comfort. Implants cost more at the start and then very little, and they rarely need replacing. Which is cheaper over a lifetime depends on how long that lifetime is and how the denture behaves. We give you the numbers for both in writing and do not pretend the upfront difference is small.

How we help you choose

  1. What do you want it to do?

    Eat anything, never think about it, keep the cost down, avoid surgery, finish quickly. Different priorities point different ways.

  2. What does your mouth allow?

    A 3D scan shows the bone. Health, medications and smoking affect implant success. Some people are better denture candidates and we say so.

  3. Is the middle ground the answer?

    For many people with a loose lower denture, two implants with a snap-in denture give most of the benefit of implants at a fraction of the cost of a full fixed arch.

  4. Can it be staged?

    A well-made denture now, with implants added under it later, is a legitimate plan. The denture is designed with that in mind.

A fair word for dentures

A well-made denture, fitted carefully and relined when needed, serves many people well for years, particularly an upper denture. Implants are not a moral upgrade. They are a better tool for some situations and an unnecessary one for others, and the consultation is where we work out which yours is.

Straight answers

Am I too old for implants?

No. Age itself is not a barrier; bone, health and medications are what matter, and they are assessed at the consultation. Implants are placed routinely for people in their eighties.

Can I have implants later if I choose dentures now?

Usually, yes, especially if the denture is planned with that in mind. Bone loss under a denture makes it somewhat harder with each passing year, so the conversation is worth having early.

Which is faster?

Dentures, by months. A conventional denture is finished in a few weeks. Implants need several months of healing before the final teeth go on, though temporaries keep you presentable throughout.

Which do most people choose?

It varies with the situation. For a single missing tooth, implants are the usual choice. For a full lower arch, the snap-in implant-retained denture is a common and very satisfying middle path. For an upper arch, a conventional denture serves many people well.
