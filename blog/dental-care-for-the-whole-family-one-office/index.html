One dental home for the whole family: what that actually means

It is on our sign, so it is worth explaining. Complete care, one dental home, is a description of how the office works rather than a slogan.

September 28, 2026·4 min read·Reviewed for patient education

The way it usually works, and why it is a nuisance

A typical family in Northern Virginia has a pediatric dentist for the children, a general dentist for the parents, an orthodontist across town for the teenager, an oral surgeon somewhere else for the wisdom teeth, and a periodontist or implant office for the grandparent. Five phone numbers, five sets of forms, five insurance verifications, records that do not talk to one another, and nobody who sees the whole picture.

Each of those offices may be excellent. The gaps between them are where things fall: the X-ray that has to be retaken, the medication nobody mentioned, the child whose bite nobody watched developing because the pediatric office stopped at twelve and the adult office started at sixteen.

What one dental home means here

Two general dentists who see every age, in one building, with one set of records. Dr. Sameer Atrash brings a preventive, conservative approach and a particular interest in restorative work, clear aligners and digital dentistry. Dr. Fadi Alhrashi brings nearly two decades of advanced training, including an Advanced General Dentistry residency and a dental implant fellowship. Between them, the office handles cleanings and sealants, fillings and crowns, root canals and extractions, aligners, dentures and implants. When a case genuinely needs a specialist, we say so and coordinate, and your records come with you.

The result is that a child's first visit, a teenager's aligner check-in, a parent's crown and a grandparent's implant-retained denture can happen on the same Saturday morning, in rooms a few steps apart.

A family over twenty years

Here is how it plays out for one household.

  1. Age one

    A first visit by the first tooth. A count, a ride in the chair, advice on bottles and brushing. Short and calm. See children's dentistry.

  2. Age six and twelve

    Sealants on the new molars as they arrive, fluoride at check-ups, and the dentist who saw the baby teeth now watching the adult ones come in.

  3. Age fourteen

    Clear aligners for crowding, planned by the dentist who already knows the bite, with check-ins after school. See aligners for teens.

  4. Age nineteen

    Wisdom teeth evaluated on the panoramic X-ray that was taken for the aligners, and removed over a college break.

  5. The parents, throughout

    Cleanings at 7 AM before the commute. A cracked molar crowned before it splits. A night guard for the grinder. Whitening before a wedding.

  6. The grandparents

    Dry mouth managed with fluoride and closer visits, a loose lower denture anchored on two implants, medications coordinated with their physicians. See care for seniors.

The practical side

  • One registration for each family member through the same online forms, with insurance uploaded once.
  • Back-to-back or overlapping appointments so one trip covers everyone. See family dentistry.
  • Aetna, Ameritas, Delta Dental, Humana, Cigna, MetLife and Virginia Medicaid all accepted, so the children's Medicaid and the parents' PPO plan are handled in the same office.
  • Care in English, Arabic, Spanish and Portuguese, which matters when a grandparent and a grandchild speak different languages at the same visit.
  • Hours from 7 AM to 8 PM on weekdays and 8 AM to 1 PM on weekends, so the visit fits the family rather than the other way round.

What it does not mean

It does not mean doing everything regardless of whether we are the best people to do it. Complex orthodontics, advanced gum surgery, deeply impacted wisdom teeth and some root canals are better in a specialist's hands, and we refer those with your records and a plain explanation. One dental home means someone is responsible for the whole picture and knows when to bring in help, not that help is never needed.

A note about online dental information

This article is educational and cannot diagnose a condition or replace an examination. Recommendations depend on your symptoms, health history and clinical findings.

Quick answers

Do I have to see the same dentist every time?

You can ask for one, and we will tell you when they are available. Both dentists work from the same records and the same approach, so whichever you see, the plan is consistent.

Can my children and I really be seen on the same morning?

Yes. Tell us how many are coming and we arrange consecutive slots. Weekend mornings are the most popular time for this.

What if a family member needs a specialist?

We refer with your records and explain why. Your general care, cleanings and follow-up stay here, and we coordinate with the specialist so nothing falls between the offices.
