Your first visit to a new dentist: a checklist

Whether you have just moved to Alexandria or are finally going back after years away, a little preparation turns the first visit into a useful one.

September 14, 2026·4 min read·Reviewed for patient education

Before you book

  • Decide what you want from the visit: a routine check-up, a specific problem looked at, a second opinion on a quoted treatment, or simply a conversation if you are nervous.
  • Check the hours fit your life. An office that is only open when you are at work will keep losing to the calendar.
  • Check they take your insurance, and ask whether that means in-network. Our insurance page lists the plans we accept, including Virginia Medicaid.
  • If language matters, ask. We offer care in English, Arabic, Spanish and Portuguese.

Getting your records transferred

  1. Your records belong to you

    Any previous dental office must provide copies of your records and X-rays on request. A modest copying fee is sometimes allowed; withholding records over a disputed bill is not.

  2. Ask for the X-rays specifically

    Recent bitewings or a panoramic image, sent digitally, can mean no new X-rays at your first visit here.

  3. Let the new office request them

    We can send the request for you once you have registered. It usually takes a few days to a couple of weeks.

  4. Do not delay the visit waiting for them

    If a tooth hurts or it has been years, come in. Records can follow.

What to bring

  • Photo ID and your insurance card, or upload a photo of the card through the online registration beforehand.
  • A complete medication list, including supplements and anything you take occasionally. Blood thinners, bone medications and diabetes drugs change how treatment is planned.
  • A note of any allergies and medical conditions, and any previous problems with anesthesia or getting numb.
  • The specific thing you want looked at, if there is one, and when it started.
  • Any treatment plan or estimate from another office, if you want a second opinion.
  • Your questions, written down. They are easy to forget in the chair.

What a thorough first exam includes

It should start with a conversation before the chair tips back: what brought you in, your dental history, your health, what you want from the visit. Then the examination: gum pockets measured around every tooth, every tooth and existing filling checked, your bite assessed, the jaw joints felt, and an oral cancer screening of the lips, tongue, cheeks, palate and throat. X-rays are taken when there is a reason, with the images shown to you on a screen. If your gums are healthy, a cleaning is usually done the same visit; if gum disease is found, a deeper cleaning is planned separately so it can be done properly. Allow about an hour to ninety minutes. Our first visit page walks through it step by step.

Questions worth asking

What did you find?

In plain language, with the image on the screen. You should be able to see the reason for any recommendation.

What needs doing now, and what can wait?

A good plan separates the urgent from the optional. Everything at once is a warning sign.

What are the alternatives?

Including doing nothing for now. Most problems have more than one reasonable answer.

What will it cost, in writing?

With your insurance checked first, so the number is real.

Who does the work?

Here, in the office, or by referral? And who follows up afterward?

What if I am nervous?

The answer tells you a lot about how the office treats people. See our comfort options.

What a written plan should contain

A list of what was found. A recommendation for each item, with the alternatives. Which items are urgent, which are advisable this year, and which can be watched. The number of visits. The fee for each item, what your insurance is expected to pay, and your estimated share. No pressure to sign anything on the day. If a plan you are handed does not have those things, ask for them before agreeing to treatment.

Warning signs of a rushed office

  • No conversation before the exam, and no one asks about your health or medications.
  • A long list of treatment with no explanation of priorities or alternatives.
  • Pressure to decide and pay the same day, or a discount that expires if you leave.
  • You never see your own X-rays.
  • Every patient seems to need the same expensive treatment.
  • Questions are met with impatience.

A note about online dental information

This article is educational and cannot diagnose a condition or replace an examination. Recommendations depend on your symptoms, health history and clinical findings.

Quick answers

How long will a first visit take?

Allow about an hour to ninety minutes for a new-patient exam, X-rays if needed and a cleaning if your gums allow it. Completing registration online beforehand saves time at the desk.

Will I get a cleaning at the first visit?

Usually, if the gums are healthy. If gum disease is found, the right treatment is a deep cleaning planned as its own appointment.

Can I get a second opinion without committing to treatment?

Yes. Bring the other office's plan. We will tell you honestly whether we would do the same, less, or something different, and why, with no obligation.
