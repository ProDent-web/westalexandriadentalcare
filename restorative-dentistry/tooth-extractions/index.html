Restore your smile

Tooth extractions in West Alexandria

We would rather save a tooth than remove one, and we say honestly when saving it is no longer sensible. When a tooth does need to come out, the goal is a calm, numb, uneventful appointment, a clear plan for the days afterward, and a decision already made about what replaces it.

When a tooth cannot be saved

  • Decay or a fracture has gone below the bone level, leaving nothing solid to build on.
  • A crack runs down the root, which no filling or crown can hold together.
  • Gum disease has destroyed so much bone that the tooth is loose and cannot be stabilized.
  • A root canal has failed and cannot be redone or treated surgically.
  • A wisdom tooth is impacted, infected or damaging its neighbor. See wisdom teeth removal.
  • A tooth has to go to make room for orthodontic treatment, or a baby tooth is blocking an adult one.

What the day is like

  1. Before

    We review your medications, especially blood thinners and bone medications, and your medical history. Eat a normal meal unless sedation instructions say otherwise. Bring someone to drive if sedation is planned.

  2. Numbing

    Topical gel, then local anesthetic given slowly. You should feel pressure during the extraction but no pain; if you feel anything sharp, raise a hand and we add more before continuing.

  3. The extraction

    The tooth is loosened gently and lifted out. The sensation is pushing and pressure. Most simple extractions take only a few minutes once numb; the preparation takes longer than the removal.

  4. Stopping the bleeding

    You bite on gauze for twenty to thirty minutes while a clot forms. Written aftercare instructions go home with you.

Aftercare that prevents dry socket

The clot in the socket is what protects the bone. Losing it causes dry socket, which is painful. These steps keep it in place.

  • Bite on gauze for the first half hour and change it only if soaked.
  • No straws, spitting, rinsing or smoking for at least seventy-two hours; suction and smoke pull the clot out.
  • Soft, cool foods for the first day. Chew on the other side.
  • From the second day, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals.
  • Take pain relief as directed before the numbness wears off, not after the pain starts.
  • Sleep with your head raised the first night. Expect some swelling and bruising for a few days.
  • Call if bleeding restarts and will not stop with pressure, if pain gets worse after day three, or if you have fever or spreading swelling.

Decide the replacement before the extraction

The best time to plan what fills the gap is before the tooth comes out. If an implant is the likely answer, the socket can be preserved with a bone graft at the time of extraction so there is enough bone later. If a bridge or partial is the plan, a temporary can be ready the same day so you are never without a front tooth. Read the replacing missing teeth guide before your visit and we will go through it together.

Dr. Fadi Alhrashi completed an Advanced General Dentistry residency and a dental implant fellowship, so surgical extractions and socket preservation are done here rather than referred out in most cases.

Anxious about having a tooth out?

Say so when you book. The appointment is planned with more time, nitrous oxide is available, and other sedation options can be discussed after a review of your health. A fair number of people are surprised afterward by how little there was to it.

Straight answers

Does having a tooth pulled hurt?

Not during the extraction, which is done fully numb. You feel pressure and pushing. Afterward, soreness for a few days is normal and is managed with over-the-counter pain relief taken on schedule.

Should I stop my blood thinner?

Usually no, and never without your physician's advice. Most routine extractions are done on patients taking anticoagulants, with local measures to control bleeding. We coordinate with your doctor when needed.

How long until I can eat normally?

Soft foods the first day, then build up over a few days as comfort allows. Avoid chewing on the extraction site for about a week.

What is dry socket?

Loss of the blood clot from the socket, exposing the bone, usually three to five days after an extraction. It causes a deep ache that pain relief does not touch. It is treated with a medicated dressing in the socket and settles within days. Following the aftercare steps makes it unlikely.

Will my insurance cover the extraction?

Most plans cover extractions as basic or oral surgery benefits at a percentage. Virginia Medicaid is accepted. We confirm your coverage and give you the cost before the appointment.
