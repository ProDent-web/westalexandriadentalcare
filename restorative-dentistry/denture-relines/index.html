Denture relines in West Alexandria

A denture that fitted beautifully three years ago and now slips is not a failed denture. The jawbone under it has changed shape, as it does under every denture, and the plate no longer matches the ridge. A reline replaces the fitting surface so it matches again. It is quicker and far cheaper than a new denture, and it is the routine maintenance dentures need.

Why dentures loosen

Bone needs load to stay. Tooth roots load the jaw; a denture resting on the gum does not, and the ridge slowly shrinks. The change is fastest in the first year after teeth are removed and continues more slowly for life. The denture, meanwhile, keeps its original shape, so a gap opens between the two. Food gets under it, it rocks when you chew, and the gum takes pressure in the wrong places and gets sore.

A reline adds new acrylic to the inside of the denture, molded to the ridge as it is now. The teeth and the outside of the denture stay the same.

Signs you need a reline

  • The denture slips when you talk, laugh or eat, or you have to hold it with your tongue.
  • Food collects under the plate.
  • Sore spots keep appearing, especially on the ridge.
  • You rely on adhesive to get through the day, or need more of it than you used to.
  • The denture clicks, or your bite feels as though it has changed.
  • It has been more than two or three years since it was last relined.

MaterialCushioned, slightly flexibleHard acrylic, like the denture
Best forThin, tender ridges; sore spots; healing after extractionsMost dentures in otherwise good condition

How a reline is done

  1. Check the denture and the gums

    A denture with worn teeth, cracks or a badly changed bite may not be worth relining, and sore gums need to settle before an impression is taken. We say which it is.

  2. Impression inside the denture

    The old fitting surface is cleaned and lightly roughened, and impression material is placed inside the denture, which is seated in your mouth so the ridge is recorded exactly.

  3. Chairside or laboratory

    A chairside reline is processed in the office and you leave with it the same day. A laboratory reline gives a denser, longer-lasting result and usually means being without the denture for most of a day. We tell you which suits your denture.

  4. Fit and adjust

    The relined denture is checked for pressure points and the bite confirmed. One short follow-up is common.

When a reline is not enough

If the teeth are worn flat, the acrylic is cracked or discolored, the bite has changed so much that the jaw is over-closing, or the denture has been relined several times already, a new denture is the better investment. For a lower denture that stays loose even after a good reline, the problem is usually the ridge rather than the fit, and implants to anchor it are worth a conversation.

Please do not use a drugstore reline kit

Do-it-yourself reline materials are thick, uneven and set with the bite in the wrong place. They trap bacteria, cause sore spots and can damage the denture so badly it cannot be properly relined afterward. The same goes for gluing a broken denture. Call and we will fix it properly, usually quickly.

Straight answers

How often should a denture be relined?

Every two to three years is typical, sooner in the first year after extractions when the ridge is changing fastest. The denture is checked at every exam and we tell you when the fit has drifted.

Will I be without my denture?

For a chairside reline, an hour or two. For a laboratory reline, usually most of a day; we schedule it in the morning so it comes back by evening where possible.

Can a partial denture be relined?

Yes. The gum-bearing parts of a partial are relined in the same way, and it is often needed because the ridge changes while the clasped teeth do not.

Is a reline covered by insurance?

Many plans cover relines at a percentage, often once in a set period. We check your benefits and give you the cost beforehand.
