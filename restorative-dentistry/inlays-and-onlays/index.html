Inlays and onlays in West Alexandria

Some cavities are too big for a filling to hold up, but the tooth still has strong walls that it would be a shame to grind away for a crown. That is the gap inlays and onlays fill. They are made in a dental laboratory from a precise impression, then bonded to the tooth, so they are stronger than a filling and keep more tooth than a crown.

Inlay, onlay, filling, crown

An inlay sits inside the tooth, between the cusps, like a filling made of ceramic. An onlay covers one or more cusps as well, which is why it is sometimes called a partial crown. Both are made outside the mouth and bonded in, so the material is harder and more precisely shaped than a filling placed directly, and the bond strengthens the remaining tooth.

The choice is driven by how much tooth is left. A small cavity gets a filling. A large one with sound walls gets an inlay or onlay. A tooth that is cracked, root-treated or has very little left gets a crown. We show you the tooth and the X-ray and explain which it is.

When an onlay is the right choice

  • A large old filling has failed and the cavity now takes up more than about half the chewing surface.
  • One cusp has broken off but the rest of the tooth is solid.
  • The tooth is a molar that takes heavy chewing force and a direct filling has already cracked once.
  • You want the strongest repair that still keeps the most natural tooth.
  • The tooth has not had a root canal and is not cracked through.

The two visits

  1. Preparation

    The tooth is numbed, the old filling and any decay removed, and the cavity shaped with smooth walls so the ceramic piece can seat cleanly. Less tooth is removed than for a crown because nothing has to be cut from the outside walls.

  2. Impression and temporary

    A precise record of the prepared tooth and the opposing teeth goes to the laboratory with a shade match. A temporary filling protects the tooth in the meantime; chew on the other side where you can.

  3. Bonding

    A week or two later the temporary comes out, the ceramic piece is tried in, the fit and bite checked, and it is bonded with a resin cement that sets hard under a light. The bite is adjusted and the edges polished.

Materials and appearance

Most inlays and onlays here are ceramic, matched to the shade of the tooth so they disappear once bonded. Ceramic is hard, stain-resistant and kind to the opposing teeth. For a very heavy grinder we talk through the trade-offs, and a night guard protects the restoration the same way it protects natural teeth.

Cost, in general terms

An onlay costs more than a filling and usually a little less than a crown, because it is laboratory-made. Many plans cover it as a major restoration at the same percentage as a crown. We check your benefits and give you a written estimate before the first visit.

Straight answers

Why not just do a bigger filling?

A large direct filling has no structural strength of its own and relies on the thin walls around it, which tend to crack under chewing. A bonded ceramic onlay holds the walls together instead of pushing them apart. For a big cavity it lasts noticeably longer.

Why not just do a crown?

A crown removes the entire outer layer of the tooth, including walls that may be perfectly sound. If those walls are strong, keeping them is better for the long life of the tooth, and an onlay lets you do that.

Is there sensitivity afterward?

Some cold sensitivity for a few days to a couple of weeks is normal and settles. A bite that feels high should be adjusted promptly; call and it takes a few minutes.

Can an onlay be done in one visit?

Ours are laboratory-made, so there are two visits with a temporary in between. The result is a precisely fitted piece checked before it is bonded for good.
