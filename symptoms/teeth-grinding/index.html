Symptom guide

Teeth grinding (bruxism)

Most people who grind their teeth do not know it. They find out from a partner who hears it, a dentist who sees the flattened edges, or a morning jaw ache they had put down to sleeping badly. Grinding at night produces forces far beyond chewing, for hours, with nothing to cushion them. Over years it wears enamel to dentin, cracks fillings and teeth, and tires the jaw. It is also very manageable.

Signs you may be grinding

  • Teeth that look flat, short or chipped at the edges; front teeth that have lost their rounded corners.
  • A sore, tired or stiff jaw on waking, or headaches in the temples in the morning.
  • Sensitivity to cold along the gum line, where enamel has flaked away.
  • Fillings, crowns or veneers that keep chipping or breaking.
  • A partner who hears grinding at night.
  • Scalloped edges on the sides of the tongue, or a ridge along the inside of the cheek.
  • Teeth that feel loose or ache for no clear reason.
  • Clenching you catch yourself doing during the day, at a desk, driving or exercising.

What is usually behind it

  • Stress and anxiety, by far the most common driver, by day and by night.
  • Sleep disruption, including sleep apnea, where the jaw clenches as the airway closes. See snoring.
  • Caffeine, alcohol, nicotine and some recreational drugs, especially in the evening.
  • Some medications, notably certain antidepressants.
  • A bite that does not meet evenly, which can provoke grinding in some people.
  • Habit, once it is established, particularly daytime clenching.

What to do right now

  1. Notice daytime clenching

    Check several times a day: are your teeth touching? They should not be, except when chewing or swallowing. Lips together, teeth apart, jaw relaxed. A sticky note on the monitor works.

  2. Wind down before bed

    Less caffeine and alcohol in the evening, and whatever helps you de-stress. Grinding tracks stress closely.

  3. Rest a sore jaw

    Soft food for a few days, moist heat on the muscles, no gum, no nail biting.

  4. Avoid a drugstore guard if you can

    Soft boil-and-bite guards are chewed on and can make clenching worse. If you must use one until your visit, a thin one for a few nights only.

  5. Book an evaluation

    Grinding is diagnosed from the wear on your teeth and your symptoms. The visit confirms it and starts the plan.

What happens at the visit

We look at the wear pattern on your teeth, which shows how and how hard you grind, and check for cracks, chipped fillings and sensitivity at the gum line. The jaw muscles and joints are felt for tenderness and clicking. We ask about sleep, stress, headaches, caffeine and medications, and about snoring. If wear is advanced, X-rays check the teeth and bone. Then we plan: nearly always a custom night guard first, with the other measures around it.

How it is treated

A custom night guard

Thin, hard acrylic made from an impression of your teeth, worn at night. It takes the wear instead of your teeth and lets the muscles relax. The single most effective measure.

Learn more

Habit awareness

For daytime clenching: reminders, posture, and the lips-together-teeth-apart rule. Simple and surprisingly effective.

Stress and sleep

Addressing what drives the grinding, from exercise and sleep habits to a conversation with your physician about anxiety or a sleep study.

TMJ care

For jaw pain, heat, exercises and short-term medication alongside the guard.

Learn more

Repairing the damage

Bonding for chipped edges, crowns or onlays for cracked teeth, desensitizing for worn necks.

Learn more

Protecting your dental work

A guard is strongly recommended after crowns, veneers or implants for anyone who grinds, because grinding is what breaks them.

Learn more

What grinding does over a decade

Year by year the edges flatten and the biting surfaces lose their shape. Enamel thins at the necks of the teeth and flakes, exposing dentin that is sensitive and decays more easily. Fillings crack. Eventually teeth crack, and a cracked tooth needs a crown or is lost. The bite collapses slightly as teeth shorten, which changes the face. None of this happens in a year, and all of it is prevented by a guard that costs a fraction of one crown.

Straight answers

Will a night guard stop me grinding?

No. It stops the grinding from damaging your teeth and usually eases the muscle pain, because the smooth surface lets the jaw slide rather than lock. The grinding itself is driven by stress, sleep and habit.

Is a drugstore guard good enough?

As a stopgap for a few nights, perhaps. Long term, soft guards are chewed on, wear through quickly and can change the bite. A custom hard guard is thin, fits precisely and protects properly.

My child grinds at night. Should I worry?

Grinding is common in young children and usually fades as the adult teeth come in. It rarely needs treatment. Mention it at check-ups so we can watch the wear; a guard is considered for teenagers with adult teeth and visible damage.

Can grinding be a sign of sleep apnea?

It can. Grinding often accompanies disrupted breathing during sleep. If you also snore, wake unrefreshed or are sleepy by day, ask your physician about a sleep study.
